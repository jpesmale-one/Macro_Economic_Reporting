Every threshold in §2 is BINARY, not a rate-of-change test. GBP Retail Sales Ex Fuel decelerated 2.7 percentage points (5.0% → 2.3%) and stayed GOOD because the test is >0.5%, and 2.3% clears it as surely as 5.0% did. GBP would only lose the point at 0.5% or below — a further 1.8pp fall.

This is a real limitation and it is worth stating plainly: the model cannot see a deceleration until it crosses a line. A currency can weaken materially for weeks while its score sits still. Drivers — the chain, in plain terms (LD-31)

1. Treasury buyback expansion — the mechanism still running. Treasury said on 19 August it will buy back at least twice as many long-dated bonds — from $2bn to $4bn per operation, effective 9 September. 3. Debasement demand. When a government expands operations that support its own bond market, some buyers read it as the currency being managed rather than left to the market, and move to assets no government can print. This is the force gold and BTC share, and it is why they moved together on Friday.
