2.7 What the score cannot see — binary thresholds

Every threshold in §2 is BINARY, not a rate-of-change test. A figure can move a long way and the grade will not budge until it crosses a line — and a small move that DOES cross one changes the score outright. Both halves showed up last night.

RowWasNowMoveThresholdScore
USD Durable Goods Ex Transp0.6%0.4%−0.2pp>0.5%GOOD → BAD
USD Personal Spending MoM0.3%0.2%−0.1pp0–0.5%NEUTRAL → NEUTRAL
AUD Inflation Rate YoY3.8%3.5%−0.3pp>2% & fallingNEUTRAL → NEUTRAL
GBP Retail Sales Ex Fuel5.0%2.3%−2.7pp>0.5%GOOD → GOOD

Durable Goods moved 0.2pp and cost USD a full point. GBP retail sales more than halved and cost nothing. The size of a move is irrelevant to §2 — only its position relative to a line matters.

A currency can weaken or firm materially for weeks while its score sits still, and it can lose a point on a rounding-scale move. The direction and the magnitude are both information the score discards.

This is why §4.6 exists, and last night made the case twice over: the tally marked USD down while the dollar rallied on an off-list PCE beat (F-55). §2 answers which side of the line the data is on. It is deliberately slow, and that is what makes it a baseline rather than a signal.

Where it matters this week: GBP is the LOW-confidence leg of GBPUSD, and one of the two indicators behind its Spending point has decelerated sharply while the score held. The score says hold; the direction says watch.

Drivers — the chain, in plain terms (LD-31)

1. Treasury buyback expansion — the mechanism still running. Treasury said on 19 August it will buy back at least twice as many long-dated bonds — from $2bn to $4bn per operation, effective 9 September. More buying pushes bond prices up, which pushes their yields down (30-year fell 9bp on the announcement). Gold pays no interest, so when bonds pay less, holding gold costs you less by comparison. Gold rose with falling yields, not against them.

2. A falling dollar. Gold is priced in dollars. The dollar hit three-month lows on its worst week since late July. When the dollar falls, the same ounce of gold costs more dollars — mechanically, before any demand changes.

3. Debasement demand. When a government expands operations that support its own bond market, some buyers read it as the currency being managed rather than left to the market, and move to assets no government can print. This is the force gold and BTC share, and it is why they moved together on Friday.
