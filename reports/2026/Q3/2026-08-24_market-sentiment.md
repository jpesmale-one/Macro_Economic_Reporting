### 🇯🇵 Japan — the intervention has NOT ended the carry trade

**⚠ This corrects the emphasis of Sunday's §1.** *(cnbc.com, 20 Aug — found in
this morning's sweep)*

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What happened** | Japanese investors net bought **more than ¥5 trillion** of foreign equities and long-term bonds in the two weeks to **15 Aug** |
| **Why** | The intervention pushed the yen from ~164 to ~155, and investors **used the stronger yen to fund new non-yen positions** |
| **The quote** | Intervention *"turbo charged"* carry trades without removing the incentive — Jesper Koll, Monex Group |
| **Where it left the yen** | Gains **faded quickly**; USDJPY is back at **158.884** |

**How this reaches the bias, plainly:** a carry trade borrows cheaply in yen and
buys higher-yielding assets abroad, which means **selling yen**. The
intervention made yen cheaper to borrow *in relative terms* by strengthening it
first — so the operation that was meant to support the yen gave carry traders a
better entry to sell it.

**For CADJPY (long CAD, short JPY):** this is **supportive**, not threatening.
The structural incentive to be short yen is intact and was reinforced. **The
intervention risk still exists** — Japan says it "will not hesitate" — but it is
a **discrete event risk**, not a trend, and the trend runs the other way.

**⚠ Sunday's edition framed the intervention as a standing threat to CADJPY.
That framing was incomplete.** The threat is real but episodic; the underlying
flow favours the position. Both belong in the read.

### 2.7 Why the tally did not move, though three figures did

**Three Friday prints were fed into the model this session and the tally is
unchanged.** That is a property of the scoring test, not a stale table.

| Row | Was | Now | Move | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GBP Retail Sales YoY | 4.2% *(Jun)* | **1.6%** *(Jul)* | **−2.6pp** | GOOD → **GOOD** |
| GBP GfK Consumer Confidence | −17 *(Jul)* | **−14** *(Aug)* | +3 | GOOD → **GOOD** |
| EUR Consumer Confidence | −15.9 *(Jul)* | **−15.5** *(Aug)* | +0.4 | GOOD → **GOOD** |

**Every threshold in §2 is BINARY, not a rate-of-change test.** GBP Retail Sales
Ex Fuel decelerated **2.7 percentage points (5.0% → 2.3%)** and stayed GOOD
because the test is `>0.5%`, and 2.3% clears it as surely as 5.0% did. GBP
would only lose the point at **0.5% or below** — a further 1.8pp fall.

**This is a real limitation and it is worth stating plainly: the model cannot
see a deceleration until it crosses a line.** A currency can weaken materially
for weeks while its score sits still. GBP's retail sales more than halving is
exactly that case — the *direction* is information the score discards.

**Where it matters this week:** GBP is the LOW-confidence leg of the **GBPUSD**
proposal. Its +5.75 is unchanged, but one of the two indicators behind its
Spending point just decelerated sharply. **The score says hold; the direction
says watch.**

**CAD could not be updated at all.** StatCan's 21 Aug release publishes **no
year-over-year figure**, and its −0.8% July number is an **advance estimate
