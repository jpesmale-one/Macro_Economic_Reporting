2 — The market analysis

The pairs made money. The reasoning behind them did not hold up.

  1. 2aThe selection, and what formed the bias

    The board put EUR +6.38, CHF +6.09, JPY +3.85 in the strong band. The set then took one tier-1 pair per risk category and per strong currency — a rule that changed twice during the week, ending at LD-126 after Jeff spotted that JPY had no pair.

    GDP entered the calibrator scored on its expectation range (LD-116/117): EUR +1 on Monday, JPY +1 on Tuesday. JPY's calibrator finished at +2.00, the largest on the board.

    What the grid says that the close did not

    AUDJPY was not one lucky day — it was three. Monday 1.32×, Tuesday 1.42×, Thursday 0.78×. A bias that delivers a full day's travel three times in a week is describing the market, not catching it.

    AUDCHF was a win, and the close called it a loss. It reached 0.90× on Thursday — 3.5 pips short of a full ATR — then finished the week at −24.1. By close-to-close it looks like the model was simply wrong about CHF. It was not: the market did travel the distance, on one day, in the direction called.

    EURUSD is the honest failure. Its best session was 0.66×, and it never came close. The widest gap on the board (+6.69) produced the only bias the market never favoured on any day.

    Thursday was the week's hinge, and it was one number

    AUD consumer inflation expectations missed at 4.9% against 5.1% (09:00 PHT). All three Thursday hits — EURAUD 1.69×, AUDCHF 0.90×, AUDJPY 0.78× — are AUD-short or AUD-crossed. One AUD miss lit up three of the four pairs on the same morning.

    The other catalysts, by day: Mon AUD Job Ads beat 2.5% vs 0.5% — which is why AUDJPY ran and AUDCHF did not; Tue JPY GDP 0.4% vs 0.3%; Wed JPY Machine Tool Orders 64.7% vs 43.0%.

    No GOLD or BTC lens this week — both were removed from the model in the revamp.

Verdict

The process got better and the analysis got lucky

The build improved materially. Actuals in the fact tables, every figure generated, timezone handling enforced by code, ten findings closed and recorded to the root.

The analysis was mostly wrong and finished up. +232 pips came almost entirely from one pair, which the model ranked last of the four, and which exists only because Jeff asked a question the rule had not anticipated. The board ranked the four strongest currencies in essentially random order.

The better yardstick did not rescue it. Grading against a typical day's range lifts the score to 3 of 4 and is the right test for an intraday read — but conviction still ranked backwards under it, at the same rho −0.40. A fairer measure that still inverts means the problem is in the scoring, not in how the outcome was counted.

Both are true and they should not be blended. A profitable week produced by the lowest-conviction pair is not evidence the conviction measure works — it is evidence it does not, and the P&L obscured that rather than revealing it.

Directional pips are not P&L. There is no position sizing anywhere in this repo — these are the moves the reads pointed at, summed, and nothing more.
