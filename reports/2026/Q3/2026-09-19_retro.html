2 — The market analysis

One read expressed three times, against a week decided by a central bank we never scored.

  1. 2aThe selection, and what formed the bias

    The board put GBP +9.19, EUR +5.50, CHF +5.09 in the strong band and AUD at 0.00, alone at the bottom. The set took one tier-1 pair per category and per strong currency.

    THE CALIBRATOR HYPOTHESIS DID NOT SURVIVE ITS FIRST INDEPENDENT TEST Last week the calibrator ranked at +0.80 while the base sat at +0.00, and that retrospective's concrete next step was: "run this decomposition on the 14–18 Sep week before trusting it." It ran at −0.24. Two observations, one strongly positive and one negative, is noise — not a mechanism. The honest reading: we still have no evidence either half of the model ranks currency strength, and last week's +0.80 should now be treated as the small sample it always was.

    The root cause is not the weights. It is the input set.

    Three central bank decisions landed inside the graded week. All three were visible in the capture we built the edition from:

    2026-09-17 02:00 PHT  US  fed interest rate decision  cons=4%      prev=3.75%
2026-09-17 19:00 PHT  GB  boe interest rate decision  cons=3.75%   prev=3.75%
2026-09-18 11:00 PHT  JP  boj interest rate decision  cons=1.25%   prev=1%

    We had the Fed's consensus and previous on file and scored nothing. The calibrator graded 20 rows that week — Machinery Orders, Building Permits, Dairy Trade — and not the 25bp hike that decided it.

    The reason is structural, and it is one of ours: LD-23 says no input may be used that cannot be sourced for all eight currencies on equal terms, and rate decisions have no numeric "actual" TE publishes uniformly. Interest Rate Expectations as its own category was parked for exactly this reason. That parking decision now has a cost attached to it.

    THE CONCRETE PROPOSAL — JEFF'S CALL (F-3) A rate decision does have a numeric surprise: actual − consensus, in basis points — and TE publishes both for every one of the eight. That satisfies LD-23 on its own terms. It would have scored USD +1 on Thursday and JPY 0 (on consensus, fully priced per LD-90), which alone flips USD above GBP on the board.

  4. 2dBest practices to continue

    Grade on the day-range, not the close. It cut the other way this week: all three pairs closed negative and reached no target, so the two measures agreed. A yardstick that only flatters results is not a yardstick — this week it confirmed the loss rather than softening it.

    Print the leg overlap. The edition said {'AUD': 3} on its face. That disclosure is what makes this week's result interpretable rather than mysterious.

    Publish the rho even when it is bad. Three weeks, three readings: −0.40, +0.00, +0.02. The model has never ranked a week.

Verdict

The instrumentation got much better and the model got exposed

Six findings closed, five new guards, two scoring rules turned from prose into executable code with 28 test cases behind them. The repo can now answer "is this row stale?" four different ways, and caught a defect before Jeff did for the first time.

And the week was a clean 0 for 3. Not unlucky — structurally wrong. All three pairs shorted the same currency, so there was one read, not three. The currency that decided the week was ranked 4th. The most confident call the model has ever made, GBP at +9.19, finished 6th of 8.

The calibrator hypothesis — the one useful thing to come out of last week — did not survive its first independent test. +0.80 became −0.24.

What this week actually bought is a precise statement of what is wrong. It is not the weights, and it is not the data quality — that is now measurably good. It is that three central banks moved and the model had no way to see any of them. That is a whitelist question, and it is Jeff's call.

Directional pips are not P&L. There is no position sizing anywhere in this repo — these are the moves the reads pointed at, summed, and nothing more.
