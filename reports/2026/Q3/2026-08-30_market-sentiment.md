### 2.7 What the score cannot see — and what changed today

**Until this morning every indicator resolved to GOOD / NEUTRAL / BAD.** Trade
and Inflation carry ONE row each, so those categories were three-way switches —
and **Inflation is the heaviest at weight 3.** It could not distinguish *"2.9%
and rising fast"* from *"2.1% and barely above target"*.

**Measured before changing anything: 26 of 48 category slots sat AT THEIR
CEILING (54%).** A maxed slot cannot register good news, only bad. **CAD was
maxed in five of six categories** and had ranked #1 for weeks while losing on
price — its score had stopped being a signal and become a constant.

**LD-71: indicators now return a graded magnitude.** Four rules cover 50 of 71
rows — `>X%`, `>Prev`, `>50.5` (PMI), `<Prev`. Scales are deliberately
conservative so an ordinary beat lands mid-range and **the category keeps
headroom. That headroom is the fix.**

| | Before | **After** |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling slots | 26 of 48 | **13** |
| CAD maxed categories | 5 of 6 | **2** |

**⚠ 21 rows KEEP three-state scoring on purpose.** Several are rules Jeff
specified by hand — **LD-57 full-time employment, LD-58 claimant count** — and
silently regrading his specification is exactly the drift **LD-59** warns
against.

**What the grading does NOT fix:** it still cannot see an indicator that is not
on the whitelist. **US PCE moved the dollar twice last week and §2 was blind
both times.** That is a whitelist question (F-3), and it is Jeff's call.

**CAD could not be updated at all.** StatCan's 21 Aug release publishes **no
year-over-year figure**; its −0.8% July number is an **advance estimate from
56.5% of surveyed firms**, to be revised. Complete July data lands **24
September** *(www150.statcan.gc.ca, primary)*. The model keeps May/26 5.9%,
**flagged, not silently carried**.
