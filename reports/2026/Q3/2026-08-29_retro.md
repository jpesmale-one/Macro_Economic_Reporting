# The week the dollar decided everything

Four pairs locked on Sunday 23 August. **One read worked.** The build got
measurably stronger while the analysis got measurably beaten — and the two facts
have the same root cause.

| | |
|---|---|
| Reads correct | **1 / 4** — EURCHF only |
| Directional sum | **−230.5** pips, not P&L |
| Falsifiers fired | 1 — AUDNZD, Thu |
| Near misses | 2 — 4.2 and 7.1 pips |
| Editions built | 7, 51 commits |
| Guards | **11 → 15** + whole-edition sweep |

## Part one — The report build

### 1a What went well

**The generators stopped drifting.** Every §2 table, tally and week-ahead block
came out of `score_model.py`, `section2.py` and `week_ahead.py`. Not once this
week did a hand-written version of a generated block ship — which was the single
most common failure of the fortnight before it.

**Sourcing moved up the chain.** Seven figures this week came from the issuing
agency rather than a calendar mirror: Census PDFs (text-extracted), BEA, the ABS
media release, the Swiss Federal Statistical Office, the Conference Board, and
the BLS release schedule. **In three separate cases the agency and the calendar
disagreed** — and the agency was right every time.

**The falsifier discipline held under real pressure.** AUDNZD spent three
sessions arguing against itself and stayed in because LD-29 says a pair leaves by
its falsifier, not by conviction. It left Thursday on the rule. *A discretionary
exit on Tuesday would have looked brilliant on Wednesday and would have destroyed
the only thing that makes this record readable.*

**Findings converted to guards the same day they were found.** Eleven findings,
six new guards, eight regression fixtures — each fixture reproducing an error
that actually shipped.

### 1b What broke, and what closed it

| Finding | What it was | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| F-45 | Building Permits held **June**'s −2.6% BAD while the published **July** figure was +5.0% GOOD. The audit exemption asserted a release date the agency contradicts | Row corrected; false exemption deleted |
| F-46 | One edition published **USD +1.75 in §2.0 and +2.25 in §5/§6**, both citing §2.0 | **R-16** — scores quoted anywhere must match the tally |
| F-51 | Fear & Greed was **5 days stale and the note blamed CNN** for an outage that never happened — the endpoint had published daily | **R-17** — capture must be ≤3 days old |
| F-52/53 | §4.4 called a **LOCKED pair REJECTED** for three editions; §4.5 used a 5-day high as a 45-bar high and **inverted a ranking** | **R-18** — whole-edition sweep, section vs section |
| F-54 | §2.7 answered a question three sessions old; §1 counted down to a release that had landed | **R-19** — tense check against §4.0 |
| F-57 | **CHF and NZD had zero calendar rows** — not "no event", no country at all. Both are legs of locked pairs | **R-20** — coverage gap fails loud |
| F-60 | §4.6 shipped **between §4.0 and §4.3**; nothing checked heading order | **R-21** — numerical order |

> **THE PATTERN WORTH KEEPING.** **Three of these were caught by Jeff reading,
> not by any guard** — the stale sentiment block, the whole-edition staleness,
> and the calendar blackout. Every one lived in a part of the report nobody
> re-derived. The guards were checking what *changed*; nothing was checking what
> was *assumed static*.

> **A SECOND PATTERN, IN THREE SEPARATE ROWS.** F-45, F-49 and F-50 were all
> cases of a **calendar label being trusted over the issuing agency**. LD-3
> existed for exactly that — the rule was right and the enforcement had gaps in
> three different places.

### 1c Where the build still needs work

- **The calendar capture is still not rebuilt.** CHF and NZD were hand-sourced
  twice this week. The country pages work and parse cleanly — wiring them in is a
  one-session job that has now been deferred twice.
- **Guard-writing needs its own test discipline.** R-21's first version **silently
  matched nothing** — a heredoc turned `\b` into a backspace character, and it
  reported "0 problems" on a file I knew was broken. Caught only because I ran it
  against the known-bad case. **Every new guard must be proven to fire before it
  is trusted to pass.**
- **Heredocs keep corrupting code.** Three separate incidents: backticks
  executing, `\n` becoming real newlines, `\b` becoming a control character.
  Assembly scripts should be written as files, always — the rule exists and I
  broke it twice.
- **The MCP chart lags symbol changes.** Twice a read returned the previous
  symbol's bars, once from a **different venue** (Vantage, not OANDA).
  `tv_health_check` after every set is now routine and should be permanent.
- **Prose ran 15.7–17.2% against a 15% target** all week.

### 1d What to keep doing

- **Publish the nulls.** §4.6 carries three "in line, no signal" rows. A record
  that only lists hits is a story, not evidence.
- **Re-derive every section, every edition** (LD-67). It found two defects the
  day it was introduced.
- **Record the finding before the fix**, with the original error retained. Every
  guard in this repo is a fossil of a real mistake.
- **Say plainly what was not done.** §7's "not done" list is what makes the rest
  of the edition trustworthy.

## Part two — The market analysis

### 2a How the four pairs were chosen

Three pairs were selected Sunday 23 August from a **leg-disjoint** scan of all
eight currencies — each currency used once, so no two pairs share an exposure.
AUDNZD was added as a fourth on Jeff's call after it initially failed the
structure test.

Confidence scored three inputs (LD-20): **§2 macro spread**, **technical
structure** (position in a 45-bar range and last-bar behaviour), and **week-ahead
projections**.

| Pair | Dir | Spread | Confidence | Result |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| **CADJPY** | LONG | 8.38 | **HIGH (+5)** — highest of the set | **−32.7** |
| EURCHF | LONG | 6.62 | LOW (+1) | **+17.8** |
| GBPUSD | LONG | 3.50 | LOW (+1) | −110.9 |
| AUDNZD | SHORT | 1.00 | LOW (+1) — added on Jeff's call | −104.7 |

> **THE UNCOMFORTABLE OBSERVATION.** **Confidence was inversely related to
> outcome.** The only HIGH-confidence pair lost; the widest macro spread (8.38)
> lost; the narrowest (1.00) lost most per unit of conviction. **The one that
> worked was a LOW (+1).** With n=4 this is not evidence that the confidence
> model is broken — but it is evidence that *macro spread alone did not predict a
> week's direction.*

### 2b Day by day: what actually moved

Pips versus the Sunday lock, sign-adjusted for direction. Negative means the read
was losing.

| Pair | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CADJPY | −57.0 | −46.2 | −67.4 | −43.0 | −32.7 |
| EURCHF | +4.2 | 0.0 | +27.8 | +14.6 | +17.8 |
| GBPUSD | −15.0 | +3.2 | −48.6 | −52.4 | −110.9 |

#### Forex — three separate causes, not one

**Monday · CADJPY −57.0, the worst single day of any pair.** The cause was **50%
US tariffs on Canada going live over the weekend** — the weekend the set was
locked. We found it Monday, and only after Jeff asked why CAD was weak. **CADJPY
then recovered +24.3 over the remaining four sessions:** the whole loss was the
gap we did not see coming.

**Wednesday · the day everything turned.** AU CPI printed 3.5% against 3.2–3.3%
priced — cooling, but *hotter than expected* — and AUDNZD jumped 82.9 pips
against the short. The same day, **US PCE beat** (0.2% vs 0.1%) and GBPUSD lost
51.8. **Neither PCE nor the surprise mechanism was visible to §2.**

**Thursday · both ALIGNED BID calls landed.** AU Household Spending 7.0% against
4.4% fired AUDNZD's falsifier. CHF payrolls 5.698M against 5.55M dented EURCHF.
**§4.6 had flagged both the day before.**

**Friday · Warsh.** *"We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to
our objective… Otherwise, we have work to do."* September hike odds to ~42%.
GBPUSD lost another 58.5 and came **within 7.1 pips** of its falsifier before
closing above it.

#### Gold and BTC — the same dollar, one shared driver

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Week |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DXY | +0.14 | −0.08 | +0.23 | 0.00 | +0.55 | +0.84 |
| XAUUSD | +41.6 | +7.8 | −64.3 | +7.4 | −147.2 | −154.7 |
| BTCUSD | +1296 | −723 | +245 | +1641 | −2703 | −244 |

**Gold did exactly what the thesis says it should — in reverse.** The standing
logic is *weak USD → strong gold and/or BTC*. The dollar was strong all week, and
gold gave back **154.7 points**, with **−147.2 on Warsh Friday alone**. Across
the five daily changes, `corr(DXY, gold) = −0.87` and `corr(DXY, GBPUSD) = −0.91`.

**BTC is the looser leg** — `corr = −0.67` — and Thursday proves it: **BTC +1641
on a flat dollar**, which the dollar channel cannot explain. It also rejected
81,468 twice before closing the week down 244.

> **STATED AS INFERENCE, NOT FACT.** These correlations are computed on **five
> daily observations**. They describe this week and are consistent with the
> mechanism; they are **not statistical evidence** of it. The honest claim is:
> *one variable — the dollar — is sufficient to explain four of the five sessions
> across all three lenses.*

### 2c How to see where price actually wants to go

> **THE CORE PROBLEM, STATED ONCE.** **The two events that drove the week were
> both off our whitelist.** Wednesday's PCE beat and Friday's Warsh speech.
> GBPUSD's −110.9 is almost entirely those two. Meanwhile **§2 marked USD *down*
> ** (+2.25 → +1.75) on a Durable Goods threshold crossing — **in the week the
> dollar was bought hard.** The tally was not wrong about its own question. It
> was answering a different question than the one price was asking.

1. **Decide on PCE.** It moved the dollar twice and §2 was blind both times.
   Adding it is a whitelist amendment and therefore Jeff's call (F-3) — but the
   evidence for it is now on the record.
2. **Weight scheduled speeches as risk, even though they score nothing.** Jackson
   Hole was flagged *on 23 August* — "moves USD, Gold and BTC; scores nothing."
   **We identified the week's largest driver a week early and had no way to let
   it affect a selection.** A speech cannot enter the tally, but it could enter
   *confidence*.
3. **Check the weekend before locking.** CADJPY's entire loss was a tariff regime
   that went live between Friday's close and Monday's open. **The selection ran
   Sunday and never asked what changed over the weekend.**
4. **Grow §4.6's sample and keep it honest.** n=5, five confirmed, zero
   contradicted — and **two of the five confirmations were off-list events**. It
   reads surprise, and surprise does not care whether an indicator is
   whitelisted.
5. **Consider a dollar-concentration check at selection.** Three of four legs
   were effectively short USD. When one currency drives four of five sessions,
   four "leg-disjoint" pairs are not four independent reads.

### 2d Best practices to carry forward

- **Falsifiers on closes, never wicks.** The rule cut both ways in one week — it
  kept AUDNZD in on Wednesday by 4.2 pips, took it out Thursday, and kept GBPUSD
  in on Friday by 7.1. That is a rule, not a preference.
- **Nothing rolls over.** Every set is re-derived from current evidence.
- **Diagnose which leg moved.** Twice this week a pair moved for the opposite
  reason to the obvious one — CADJPY rose Tuesday because the yen fell faster,
  and recovered Thursday because CAD genuinely firmed.
- **Separate direction from surprise** and say which one you are trading.

## The verdict, without softening

The process improved a great deal and the analysis was mostly wrong. Both are
true and they should not be blended into a single comfortable sentence.

**Guards went 11 → 15. Every figure traced to an issuing agency. And the reads
still went 1 for 4** — because the currency that mattered moved on indicators the
whitelist does not carry.

A better-instrumented report that is still wrong is not a success. What the
instrumentation bought is the ability to say *precisely why* it was wrong, one
week in, with the receipts attached. **The next week has to answer the question
this one raised: can the whitelist see what moves price?**
