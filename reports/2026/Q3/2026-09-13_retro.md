## 2 — The market analysis

*The pairs made money. The reasoning behind them did not hold up.*

### 2a The selection, and what formed the bias

The board put **EUR +6.38, CHF +6.09, JPY +3.85** in the strong band. The set
then took one tier-1 pair per risk category *and* per strong currency — a rule
that changed twice during the week, ending at LD-126 after Jeff spotted that JPY
had no pair.

**GDP entered the calibrator** scored on its expectation range (LD-116/117):
EUR +1 on Monday, JPY +1 on Tuesday. JPY's calibrator finished at **+2.00, the
largest on the board.**

### 2b Day by day — did the market favour the bias?

> **HOW THIS IS SCORED — CHANGED THIS WEEK.** A day counts when the bias
> travelled 0.75 of that pair's typical daily range, measured from that session's
> own open (LD-128/129). **Not the close.** A read that runs its way and then
> reverses did everything it was asked to do — the close measures when you
> stopped looking, not whether the bias described the day. ATR(14) is taken from
> the sessions *ending before* this week, so the yardstick is not built from the
> outcome it grades.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| EURUSD | 35p | 0.49× | 0.28× | 0.66× | 0.22× | 0.08× | no day |
| EURAUD | 52p | 0.09× | 0.38× | 0.45× | **1.69×** | 0.05× | FAVOURED |

| **AUDJPY** | 59p | **1.32×** | **1.42×** | 0.62× | **0.78×** | 0.61× | **3 of 5 days** |

**3 of 4 biases were favoured on at least one day. 5 of 20 pair-days reached the
target — 25%.**

**AUDJPY was not one lucky day — it was three.** Monday 1.32×, Tuesday 1.42×,
Thursday 0.78×. A bias that delivers a full day's travel three times in a week is
describing the market, not catching it.

**AUDCHF was a win, and the close called it a loss.** It reached **0.90×** on
Thursday — 3.5 pips short of a full ATR — then finished the week at −24.1. By
close-to-close it looks like the model was simply wrong about CHF. It was not:
the market did travel the distance, on one day, in the direction called.

**EURUSD is the honest failure.** Its best session was 0.66×, and it never came
close. The widest gap on the board (**+6.69**) produced the only bias the market
never favoured on any day.

#### Thursday was the week's hinge, and it was one number

**AUD consumer inflation expectations missed at 4.9% against 5.1%** (09:00 PHT).
All three Thursday hits — EURAUD 1.69×, AUDCHF 0.90×, AUDJPY 0.78× — are
AUD-short or AUD-crossed. **One AUD miss lit up three of the four pairs on the
same morning.**

The other catalysts, by day: **Mon** AUD Job Ads beat 2.5% vs 0.5% — which is why
AUDJPY ran and AUDCHF did not; **Tue** JPY GDP 0.4% vs 0.3%; **Wed** JPY Machine
Tool Orders **64.7% vs 43.0%**.

*No GOLD or BTC lens this week — both were removed from the model in the revamp.*

### 2c How do we foresee better?

> **⚠ THE BOARD DID NOT RANK REALISED STRENGTH.** Measured against each
> currency's move vs USD, Monday open to Friday close:

| CCY | Board | Actual vs USD | Board rank | Actual rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| EUR | +6.38 | −0.12% | 1 | 2 |
| **CHF** | +6.09 | −0.81% | 2 | **4** |
| **JPY** | +3.85 | +1.59% | 3 | **1** |
| AUD | +0.50 | −0.42% | 4 | 3 |

**rho +0.00. Every currency is in the wrong position.** CHF, ranked second
strongest, was the weakest of the four. JPY, ranked third, was the strongest by a
wide margin.

**This is a deeper finding than the pair-level correlation.** Pairs can be
mis-ranked by which pairing survived selection. A board uncorrelated with
realised strength means the scoring itself did not describe the week.

#### The decomposition — and its honest limit

| Ranking by | rho vs realised | Order produced |
|---|---:|---|
| Base only | +0.00 | EUR > CHF > JPY > AUD |
| **Calibrator only** | **+0.80** | JPY > EUR > CHF > AUD |
| Final (base + calibrator) | +0.00 | EUR > CHF > JPY > AUD |
| Actual | — | JPY > EUR > AUD > CHF |

> **STRESS-TESTED BEFORE PUBLISHING.** The forward-looking half ranked the week;
> the backward-looking half did not. That is the most useful thing in this review
> — and it rests on **four currencies over one week**. With four items there are
> 24 possible orderings, so rho takes one of a handful of values. **This
> describes the week. It is not evidence of the mechanism**, and one more week
> could move it either way.

**The concrete next step:** run this decomposition on the 14–18 Sep week before
trusting it. If the calibrator ranks again and the base does not, the weights are
the thing to change — not the indicator list.

### 2d Best practices to continue

**Grade against a typical day's range, not the close** (LD-128/129) — new this
week and it changed a verdict. **AUDCHF reads as a −24.1 loss on the close and as
a favoured bias on the grid**, because the market did travel 0.90× of a normal
day in the called direction. A trader watching that would have been out at the
target; only the retrospective was still holding it to Friday.

**The day-by-day view with catalysts attached** (LD-127) is what made Thursday
legible — one AUD miss on the same line as the three pairs it moved.

**Keep the ATR from BEFORE the week.** Building the benchmark from the graded
week's own bars would flatter every result, the same hindsight shape as F-83 and
the CHF vintage error.

## Verdict

### The process got better and the analysis got lucky

**The build improved materially.** Actuals in the fact tables, every figure
generated, timezone handling enforced by code, ten findings closed and recorded
to the root.

**The analysis was mostly wrong and finished up.** +232 pips came almost entirely
from one pair, which the model ranked last of the four, and which exists only
because Jeff asked a question the rule had not anticipated. **The board ranked the
four strongest currencies in essentially random order.**

**The better yardstick did not rescue it.** Grading against a typical day's range
lifts the score to 3 of 4 and is the right test for an intraday read — but
conviction still ranked backwards under it, at the same **rho −0.40**. **A fairer
measure that still inverts means the problem is in the scoring, not in how the
outcome was counted.**

**Both are true and they should not be blended.** A profitable week produced by
the lowest-conviction pair is not evidence the conviction measure works — it is
evidence it does not, and the P&L obscured that rather than revealing it.
