🇺🇸 US dollar — lowest since MAY, and it is the buyback again

⚠ The single most important item on the board today. (investinglive.com, Eamonn Sheridan, ~06:00 PHT; HSBC cited)

The dollar has slipped to its lowest level since May as the US Treasury doubles its long-dated bond buybacks. HSBC attributes it to falling real yields and a narrower rate gap with the euro, and sees scope for further near-term depreciation.

This is the same mechanism as 19 August, now extended. Treasury announced the buyback expansion on 19 Aug (sb0607, effective 9 September); it has now been doubled again. The mechanism, in plain terms: Treasury buying its own long bonds pushes their prices up and yields down; lower real yields make the dollar less attractive to hold and non-yielding assets cheaper to own.

Every threshold in §2 is BINARY, not a rate-of-change test. GBP Retail Sales Ex Fuel decelerated 2.7 percentage points (5.0% → 2.3%) and stayed GOOD because the test is >0.5%, and 2.3% clears it as surely as 5.0% did. GBP would only lose the point at 0.5% or below — a further 1.8pp fall.

This is a real limitation and it is worth stating plainly: the model cannot see a deceleration until it crosses a line. A currency can weaken materially for weeks while its score sits still. GBP's retail sales more than halving is exactly that case — the direction is information the score discards.

Where it matters this week: GBP is the LOW-confidence leg of the GBPUSD proposal. Its +5.75 is unchanged, but one of the two indicators behind its Spending point just decelerated sharply. The score says hold; the direction says watch.

Drivers — the chain, in plain terms (LD-31)

1. Treasury buyback expansion — the mechanism still running. Treasury said on 19 August it will buy back at least twice as many long-dated bonds — from $2bn to $4bn per operation, effective 9 September. More buying pushes bond prices up, which pushes their yields down (30-year fell 9bp on the announcement). Gold pays no interest, so when bonds pay less, holding gold costs you less by comparison. Gold rose with falling yields, not against them.

2. A falling dollar. Gold is priced in dollars. The dollar hit three-month lows on its worst week since late July. When the dollar falls, the same ounce of gold costs more dollars — mechanically, before any demand changes.

3. Debasement demand. When a government expands operations that support its own bond market, some buyers read it as the currency being managed rather than left to the market, and move to assets no government can print. This is the force gold and BTC share, and it is why they moved together on Friday.
