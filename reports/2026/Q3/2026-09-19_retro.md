## 2 — The market analysis

### 2a The selection, and what formed the bias

The board put **GBP +9.19, EUR +5.50, CHF +5.09** in the strong band and **AUD at
0.00**, alone at the bottom. The set took one tier-1 pair per category and per
strong currency, producing GBPAUD, EURAUD and AUDCHF.

> **ALL THREE PAIRS WERE SHORT AUD.** `leg_overlap` reported `{'AUD': 3}` and the
> edition printed it as a flagged note. **LD-126 dropped leg-disjointness
> deliberately** — but the consequence is that this week was not three reads, it
> was **one read expressed three times.** When AUD rose, everything lost together.

### 2b Day by day — what actually moved

| Day | Event (PHT) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Wed 16, 14:00 | **GBP CPI** 3.1% vs 3.1% cons | on consensus — priced |
| **Thu 17, 02:00** | **FED HIKED to 4.00%** from 3.75% | **rate projections raised to 4.1%** |
| Thu 17, 19:00 | **BoE held 3.75%**, 3/9 voting hike | hawkish hold |
| **Fri 18, 11:00** | **BoJ HIKED to 1.25%** from 1.00% | on consensus |

**The Fed is the week.** USD finished **+0.74%**, the only currency up against
the basket, and it was ranked **4th** on our board. Everything else fell against
it.

**GBP is the sharpest miss.** Ranked **1st at +9.19** — the widest conviction the
model has ever printed — and it finished **6th of 8, at −0.79%**, falling harder
than AUD on every single day of the week. A hawkish BoE hold did not save it.

**AUD, ranked last at 0.00, finished 2nd at −0.22%.** Its own employment data was
weak (−15.8K) and it still outperformed six currencies, because the week was not
about AUD at all.

**JPY hiked and finished last (−1.58%).** LD-90 says a decision on consensus is
fully priced; the BoJ's was, and JPY sold anyway.

> **THE CALIBRATOR HYPOTHESIS DOES NOT SURVIVE ITS FIRST INDEPENDENT TEST.**
> Last week the calibrator ranked at **+0.80** while the base sat at +0.00, and
> the retrospective's concrete next step was: *"run this decomposition on the
> 14–18 Sep week before trusting it."* **It ran at −0.24.** Two observations, one
> strongly positive and one negative, is noise — not a mechanism. The honest
> reading is that **we still have no evidence either half of the model ranks
> currency strength**, and last week's +0.80 should now be treated as the small
> sample it always was.

**THE ROOT CAUSE IS NOT THE WEIGHTS. IT IS THE INPUT SET.**

Three central bank decisions landed inside the graded week. All three were
**visible in the capture we built the edition from**:

```
2026-09-17 02:00 PHT  US  fed interest rate decision  cons=4%      prev=3.75%
2026-09-17 19:00 PHT  GB  boe interest rate decision  cons=3.75%   prev=3.75%
2026-09-18 11:00 PHT  JP  boj interest rate decision  cons=1.25%   prev=1%
```

**We had the Fed's consensus and previous on file and scored nothing.** The
calibrator graded 20 rows that week — Machinery Orders, Building Permits, Dairy
Trade — and not the 25bp hike that decided the week.

The reason is structural, and it is one of ours: **LD-23** says no input may be
used that cannot be sourced for all eight currencies on equal terms, and rate
decisions have no numeric "actual" TE publishes uniformly. **Interest Rate
Expectations as its own category was parked for exactly this reason.** That
parking decision now has a cost attached to it.

**The concrete proposal, for Jeff's call (F-3):** a rate decision *does* have a
numeric surprise — `actual − consensus` in basis points — and TE publishes both
for every one of the eight. That satisfies LD-23 on its own terms. It would have
scored USD **+1 on Thursday** and JPY **0** (on consensus, fully priced per
LD-90), which alone flips USD above GBP on the board.

### 2d Best practices to continue

**Grade on the day-range, not the close.** It cut the other way this week: all
three pairs closed negative *and* reached no target, so the two measures agreed.
**A yardstick that only flatters results is not a yardstick** — this week it
confirmed the loss rather than softening it.

**Print the leg overlap.** The edition said `{'AUD': 3}` on its face. That
disclosure is what makes this week's result interpretable rather than mysterious.

**Publish the rho even when it is bad.** Three weeks, three readings: −0.40,
+0.00 (re-scored), +0.02. The model has never ranked a week.

## Verdict

### The instrumentation got much better and the model got exposed

**Six findings closed, five new guards, two scoring rules turned from prose into
executable code with 28 test cases behind them.** The repo can now answer "is
this row stale?" in four different ways and caught a defect before Jeff did for
the first time.

**And the week was a clean 0 for 3.** Not unlucky — **structurally wrong**. All
three pairs shorted the same currency, so there was one read, not three. The
currency that decided the week was ranked 4th. The most confident call the model
has ever made, GBP at +9.19, finished 6th of 8.

**The calibrator hypothesis — the one useful thing to come out of last week —
did not survive its first independent test.** +0.80 became −0.24.

**What this week actually bought:** a precise statement of what is wrong. It is
not the weights, and it is not the data quality — that is now measurably good.
**It is that three central banks moved and the model had no way to see any of
them.** That is a whitelist question, and it is Jeff's call.
