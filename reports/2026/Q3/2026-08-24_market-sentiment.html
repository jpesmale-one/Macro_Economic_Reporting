🇯🇵 Japan — the intervention has NOT ended the carry trade

⚠ This corrects the emphasis of Sunday's §1. (cnbc.com, 20 Aug — found in this morning's sweep)

Detail
What happenedJapanese investors net bought more than ¥5 trillion of foreign equities and long-term bonds in the two weeks to 15 Aug
WhyThe intervention pushed the yen from ~164 to ~155, and investors used the stronger yen to fund new non-yen positions
The quoteIntervention "turbo charged" carry trades without removing the incentive — Jesper Koll, Monex Group
Where it left the yenGains faded quickly; USDJPY is back at 158.884

How this reaches the bias, plainly: a carry trade borrows cheaply in yen and buys higher-yielding assets abroad, which means selling yen. The intervention made yen cheaper to borrow in relative terms by strengthening it first — so the operation that was meant to support the yen gave carry traders a better entry to sell it.

For CADJPY (long CAD, short JPY): this is supportive, not threatening. The structural incentive to be short yen is intact and was reinforced. The intervention risk still exists — Japan says it "will not hesitate" — but it is a discrete event risk, not a trend, and the trend runs the other way.

⚠ Sunday's edition framed the intervention as a standing threat to CADJPY. That framing was incomplete. The threat is real but episodic; the underlying flow favours the position. Both belong in the read.

Every threshold in §2 is BINARY, not a rate-of-change test. GBP Retail Sales Ex Fuel decelerated 2.7 percentage points (5.0% → 2.3%) and stayed GOOD because the test is >0.5%, and 2.3% clears it as surely as 5.0% did. GBP would only lose the point at 0.5% or below — a further 1.8pp fall.

Drivers — the chain, in plain terms (LD-31)

1. Treasury buyback expansion — the mechanism still running. Treasury said on 19 August it will buy back at least twice as many long-dated bonds — from $2bn to $4bn per operation, effective 9 September. More buying pushes bond prices up, which pushes their yields down (30-year fell 9bp on the announcement). Gold pays no interest, so when bonds pay less, holding gold costs you less by comparison. Gold rose with falling yields, not against them.

2. A falling dollar. Gold is priced in dollars. The dollar hit three-month lows on its worst week since late July. When the dollar falls, the same ounce of gold costs more dollars — mechanically, before any demand changes.

3. Debasement demand. When a government expands operations that support its own bond market, some buyers read it as the currency being managed rather than left to the market, and move to assets no government can print. This is the force gold and BTC share, and it is why they moved together on Friday.
