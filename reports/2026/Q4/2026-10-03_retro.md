## 1 — The report build

### 1a. What went well

**R-46 ran clean before the edition and caught real work.** Eleven steps,
stopped the build twice, both times correctly: six rows from the prior week
had not been loaded. That is the checklist doing exactly what it was built
for after F-132.

**LD-146 and LD-147 both shipped inside the week they were asked for**, with
tests, and neither broke anything downstream. R-44 went 31 → 45 cases.

**The AUD employment split held up.** `39.5K (FT -6.3K, PT +45.8K)` graded BAD
on the full-time leg per LD-57, and AUD did finish the week weak. That is one
observation, not a proof — but it is the first live week where the F-138 fix
was load-bearing.

### 1b. Problems seen and resolved

| Finding | What it was |
|---|---|
| **F-140** | **R-46 step 9 was reading LAST WEEK'S PAGE.** Hardcoded `2026-09-20_weekly.html`. It passed on V1 by coincidence and then failed against a *correct* page. A guard that names the wrong file does not weaken to a no-op — it accuses correct work. |
| **The rate row printed a dash** | The engine scored the RBA −1 while the page showed `—` in both the Threshold and Score columns. Three separate causes, all live on V3. |
| **`raw[-3:]` read "ION"** | The collision table took the last three characters of the event name as the reference month, so *Interest Rate Dec**ision*** printed `ION`. On the one row LD-146 exists to explain. |
| **Wrong rule name above a correct score** | The collision label was a two-way if/else that could only say RULE 1 or RULE 2, so rate precedence rendered as *"same month, shared"* when the decision had taken the day outright. |

**The through-line: four of these are DISPLAY defects on CORRECT data.** The
model knew the right answer in every case and the page said something else.
That is a new failure class for this repo — the previous twelve findings were
mostly wrong values — and it is harder to catch because no value check fires.

### 1c. What to improve

**1. Rate rows are invisible to the display layer, repeatedly.** Three separate
findings in two weeks — `hot()` in F-135, precedence in LD-146, the threshold
column this week — all have one cause: **rate rows live in `rate_model` /
`week_ahead` while nearly every renderer resolves through `score_model`.** A
row-level lookup that knows about both would kill the class instead of the
instances.

**2. `rate_model` is hand-written and nothing checks it against TE.** The RBA
hiked to 4.60% on 29 Sep; the cell still reads 4.35%. R-46 step 6 verifies the
*page* matches `rate_model` — it never verifies `rate_model` matches the world.
**All eight rates could drift and every guard would stay green.** R-47 is the
fix: sweep the eight cells against their TE country pages, same shape as R-45.

**3. Two R-45 slugs point at the wrong series.** `CB Consumer Confidence` →
University of Michigan (a different survey), `NZD Card Spending YoY` → levels in
NZD millions. Both currently report NO PAGE, and **that is the only reason
neither has produced a bad value.** If either page had parsed, R-45 would have
flagged our correct 89.4 as stale against Michigan's 48.1.

**4. The §4 terms column still describes a retired model.** It prints
`expected +0.50 + surprise +0.00` — LD-142 retired both terms in favour of
`realised 0.2 · level 0.4 · path 0.4`. The *scores* are right; the working
beside them is a model we no longer run. **A stale explanation on a correct
number cannot be caught by a value check.**

**5. The `pair_swap.LIVE` overlay must die at the next build.** It exists only
because the base is on Sunday's vintage while the swap reads today's rate. Once
`rate_model` holds 4.60%, the AUD entry has to go — an overlay that outlives
its discrepancy becomes a second source of truth (F-127's shape).

### 1d. What to continue

- **Running R-46 first, every Sunday.** It has now stopped a bad build three
  weeks running.
- **Writing the retrospective to the repo before publishing** (LD-138).
- **Recording withdrawal conditions with each new rule**, so a rule that ranks
  nothing comes out instead of accumulating.
- **Doubting the check when it produces an implausible finding.** F-134 taught
  it; F-140 was the one-finding version and I still doubted the page first.

### 2c. How to foresee better

**The board ranked at +0.26 — the weakest of the five weeks measured.**

| Week | rho (final) | rho (base) |
|---|---|---|
| 31 Aug–4 Sep | −0.40 | — |
| 7–11 Sep | +0.00 | — |
| 14–18 Sep | +0.02 | — |
| 21–25 Sep | +0.17 | **+0.48** |
| **28 Sep–2 Oct** | **+0.26** | **+0.31** |

> **The 21–25 Sep row is the CORRECTED one.** That retrospective was first
> graded on Yahoo bars and republished on OANDA (F-137); the regrade moved base
> rho +0.29 → **+0.48** and final +0.12 → +0.17. Quoting the superseded figures
> here would have made this week look like an improvement on the base when it
> is the opposite.

**So the base FELL this week, +0.48 → +0.31**, and it still beats the final for
the second week running. LD-144 cut the calibrator to 0.2/day precisely because
it was dragging the ranking down, and it is *still* dragging — just less.
**The calibrator has never once improved the ranking in five weeks.**

**⚠ NONE OF THIS IS STATISTICALLY MEANINGFUL, and the 4 Sep retrospective said
so first:** with n=8, rho needs roughly **±0.71** for p<0.05. Every reading in
that table is indistinguishable from chance. Five weeks of consistent direction
is worth watching; it is not yet evidence, and treating +0.31 as "better than
+0.26" would be reading noise.

**EUR is the single biggest ranking error.** Ranked second at +6.00, finished
**dead last** on realised strength at −0.58%. Nothing in the macro base saw
it coming. Worth a specific look before next Sunday rather than a general
resolution to do better.

**LD-146 changed no pairing this week, and that is its withdrawal condition
speaking.** The set with LD-146 and the set without it are *the same four
pairs* — AUD moved +0.27 → −0.13, which widened every gap but reordered
nothing:

| | With LD-146 | Without |
|---|---|---|
| GBPAUD | +6.93 | +6.53 |
| EURAUD | +6.13 | +5.73 |
| GBPUSD | +5.00 | +5.00 |

The rule was right about direction — AUD *was* weak and the hike *did not*
rescue it. But **it ranked nothing**, and LD-113 and LD-140 are both precedents
for a rule withdrawn on exactly that. **One week is not a verdict.** The
12–16 Oct retrospective is where this gets decided, and the condition is
already recorded.

**LD-147's first outing was informative in a way I did not expect.** All four
pairs carried negative swap, and the reason is structural rather than
coincidental: the board ranks on macro strength, and this week the strong
currencies (GBP 3.75%, EUR 2.65%, JPY 1.25%) all carry *lower* policy rates
than the weak one (AUD 4.60%). **A macro-divergence model will systematically
select carry-negative positions.** That is not a defect and it does not argue
against the set — but it is a standing property of the method worth knowing,
and it only became visible once the column existed.

### 2d. Best practices to continue

- **Grading on the day's range, not the close.** EURAUD is this week's proof:
  −56.3 on the close, 1.32× on Wednesday.
- **Disclosing leg overlap as concentration rather than dressing it as
  diversification** (F-128).
- **Naming the event risk before the week starts.** The RBA warning was on the
  page Sunday; the trade was not a surprise on Tuesday.
- **Publishing the five-column grid**, not a win/loss count. AUDJPY hit on 3 of
  5 days and GBPAUD on 2 — the weekly figure cannot tell those apart.

## What would change my mind

**On the calibrator:** if 5–9 Oct shows the final beating the base, the
LD-144 weighting is working and the trend reverses. If the base wins a third
time, the calibrator needs a harder look than a weight change.

**On LD-146:** if it changes a pairing by 16 Oct, it stays. If it has still
ranked nothing, it comes out — LD-113's precedent.

**On the set:** four from four is a good week by the measure we chose. It is
also one AUD read expressed three times, and a week where AUD had risen would
have read very differently. **The method has not yet been tested on a week
where its concentrated leg went the wrong way.**
