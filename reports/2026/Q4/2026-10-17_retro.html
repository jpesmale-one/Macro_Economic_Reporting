The Week The Board Was Right

4 WIN of 4, set ratio 1.65 — the first clean sweep. And for the first time the ranking itself carried signal: rho +0.52 against a six-week mean of −0.10. But 43% of the week's favourable movement came from one session, and 51% of the adverse came from another. This is the first retro built on daily evidence, and the dailies change what can be concluded.

§1 — The verdict

Four of four, and the margin is not marginal. The tightest pair (CADJPY, 1.57) still clears the 1.40 band by 12%. Compare the two prior editions: 5–9 Oct returned 0 WIN, 2 CONTESTED, 2 LOSS, −245 pips, and 28 Sep–2 Oct was no better. This is the first week where the measured verdict and the intended bias agree on every pair.
⚠ CADJPY never produced a single CLEAN or BEATEN session. Five DRIFTs in a row — it won on accumulation, not on any day reaching target. Its ATR is the smallest in the set (77 pips) and its daily excursions the smallest. A 1.57 ratio built entirely from drift is a weaker claim than the same number built from two CLEAN days, and LD-149 cannot distinguish the two. Worth knowing before trusting the ratio alone.

⚠ BUT FOUR SESSIONS ARE POSITIVE AND ONE IS STRONGLY NEGATIVE. Per-session rho: Mon +0.29, Tue +0.31, Wed −0.55, Thu +0.40, Fri +0.33.

Wednesday is the day GBP ranked #8 from a board rank of #1 and JPY ranked #1 from #8 — both extremes exactly inverted, seven places each. That is also the day the set took three BEATEN. The relationship is not weak-but-steady; it is strong on four days and sharply wrong on the fifth. One week does not retire a six-week null result, and the right conclusion is that the sample now has one strong positive in it, not that the board has been shown to rank.

§4 — What the dailies proved about the catalysts

Each daily graded all eight currencies against the scheduled releases that landed that session. Across four dailies that is 32 currency-sessions — the first time this has been countable.

⚠ THE SCHEDULED DATA EXPLAINED THE MOVE 10 TIMES AND CONTRADICTED IT 8 TIMES. That is 56% — barely better than a coin toss, on the very inputs the entire base score is built from.

Thursday is the sharpest case: four consistent and four AGAINST, an exact split on the week's best session. USD retail sales beat 0.8% vs 0.1% and the dollar fell. EUR industrial production missed and the euro rose. CAD manufacturing sales beat and CAD was flat-to-lower.

This does not invalidate the board — the board scores a stock of conditions, not a day's flow. But it is direct evidence that a single session's releases are close to uninformative about that session's direction, and the daily report is now the thing that demonstrates it.

§5 — The AUD print that did the work, and the rule that disagrees

Thursday supplied 43% of the week's favourable movement, and GBPAUD was its widest pair (+1.36% in one session). Nearly all of that is one release.

The market sold AUD 1.083%. Our rule says NEUTRAL. LD-57/LD-151 grades AUD on the full-time leg, and because headline and full time disagree, employment_grade() returns NEUTRAL. I called the function rather than assuming it.

So the daily's “consistent” label on that row is the headline's verdict, not the rule's. We were long GBPAUD and were paid by a print our own scoring rule would have called neutral. That is worth more than a win: it is the first live instance of the case LD-151 was written for, and it says the rule and the market can read the same release in opposite directions.

Which is right is genuinely open. The full-time preference exists because full-time jobs carry the income that drives consumption, and that reasoning has not been falsified by one session. But a rule that says NEUTRAL on a 59K headline miss will keep diverging from price, and the divergence should be tracked rather than resolved by argument.

§6 — A metric that cannot fail is not a metric

⚠ LD-162 RETURNED “BROAD” OR “MOSTLY BROAD” IN 40 OF 40 CURRENCY-SESSIONS. NARROW NEVER ONCE FIRED.

The cause is not the market. Breadth is measured only against counterparts we hold bars for: USD gets 7, JPY 4, GBP 3, CAD/EUR/AUD 2, and CHF and NZD get 1. With one counterpart the question “did it move against most of them?” has no content — “BROAD, stronger against all 1” is arithmetically true and says nothing.

LD-162 was added for a real reason, and it did work where the data was thick: on Tuesday it correctly showed the whole board was one USD move seen from seven sides, and every other currency's best gain was against USD. The metric is sound; its input is too thin for five of eight currencies. The fix is the full 28-pair grid rather than 11 pairs, and until then a BROAD reading on n=1 should be treated as no reading at all.

That concentration then cost three BEATEN on Wednesday — and the 12–16 Oct edition still carried a note saying the overlap was “balanced for the first time in a month”, written before the rotation and never revisited.

The set changed; the diversification claim did not. That is the same family as F-149/F-151 — a description outliving the rule it described — except in prose rather than a table column, where no guard looks at all.
⚠ AND THE CONCENTRATION IS NOW A MEASURED LOSS, NOT A HYPOTHETICAL. Three of four pairs were short JPY. On Wednesday JPY ranked #1 of eight and the set took three BEATEN in one session — 51% of the week's entire adverse movement. The week still won, because Thursday was large enough to absorb it. That is luck about magnitude, not a defence of the concentration.

§8 — What this says for next week

Does the board rank? Unresolved, but better evidenced than it has ever been. rho +0.52 is one strong positive in a seven-week sample whose mean is still near zero. The useful next step is not another week of rho — it is asking why Wednesday was −0.55 when four other sessions were positive.

Is the set too concentrated? Yes, on evidence. ×3 on one leg cost three BEATEN in a single session. A cap of ×2 per leg would have excluded one pair this week — and would also have excluded GBPAUD, the best performer. The trade-off is real and it is Jeff's call, not a derivation.

Are the scheduled releases informative? Less than assumed. 10 consistent against 8 AGAINST over 32 currency-sessions. They may still be informative about the stock of conditions while being near-useless about a single day's direction — which is exactly what the base/calibrator split already assumes, now with a number attached.

What should be fixed first? The 28-pair bar grid. It costs nothing in logic, it makes LD-162 able to return NARROW, and without it five of eight currencies have a breadth reading that cannot be wrong and therefore cannot be right.

The one thing I would not conclude from this week. Four of four won, rho turned positive, and the bias was paid on the session it mattered. It is tempting to read that as the method working. But 43% of the gain came from one print that our own rule grades NEUTRAL, and the week's worst session was the one where the board's two extreme ranks both inverted. A good week with a known mechanism of failure inside it is still a good week — it is just not yet evidence of skill.
