The diversified leg was the worst performer in the set. GBPAUD took two BEATEN — more than any CHF pair — and finished the only pair with more adverse than favourable movement.

This does not make the warning wrong. Thursday was exactly the predicted failure: CHF rose against all four counterparts and took three BEATEN in one session, 32% of the week's adverse in a single day. The risk was real and it fired. It was simply outweighed by four sessions where the same concentration paid three times over — the CHF leg ran 4.02, 9.19, 3.01 and 1.85 on the other four days.

What this actually argues is that ×3 is a variance decision, not a quality one. It makes good weeks better and bad days worse. Two weeks of evidence now point in opposite directions — 14 Oct cost three BEATEN inside a winning week, 22 Oct cost three BEATEN inside a CONTESTED one — and neither settles it.

Drop-one testing: GBP +0.15, CAD +0.06, NZD +0.08, JPY +0.20, USD +0.15, EUR +0.10, CHF +0.00 — and AUD +0.81. Every other currency moves rho by at most 0.17. AUD moves it by 0.63.

AUD was board #8 and traded like #2.6. It ranked #1 or #2 on three sessions where it had zero scheduled releases, and ranked #6 on the one day it did have data — a flash PMI miss at 47.8 against 51.1.
And the board is not wrong about AUD's fundamentals. I checked whether the negative rows were stale: they are not. Production (−2) rests on a Sep PMI of 49.6, and the October flash printed 49.7 on Thursday — still contractionary, still confirming. Trade (−2) rests on an Aug balance that does not re-print until 5 Nov. The data says AUD is weak. Price disagreed for four of five sessions.

That gap is the single most useful thing this week produced. It is not a data-freshness problem, so it cannot be fixed by refreshing; it is either flow the board cannot see, or a genuine limit on what fundamentals predict over five days.

§6 — What the dailies caught

The CHF warning was dated and specific one session before it fired. Wednesday's daily carried: EUR/CHF at a three-month low, “the Swiss franc draws renewed support from its traditional safe-haven appeal”, French 10-year yields above 5%, the France–Germany spread near 170bp, and the explicit line “three of our four pairs are short CHF”. Thursday it happened.

The franc's strength is euro weakness, not a Swiss story — and our board scores CHF on Swiss fundamentals at −1.20, so it structurally cannot see it. That is a named, reproducible blind spot rather than bad luck.
Two prints read against their own move, both on our main long. Tuesday's UK jobs report beat on every stock measure (unemployment 4.7% vs 4.9%, employment +102K vs +20K, payrolls +13K vs −15K) and missed badly on the one flow measure (claimant count 30.3K vs 19.4K). Wednesday's UK CPI beat on headline, core and MoM — hawkish under LD-155 — and GBP fell anyway. GBP finished the week at board #1 and session-mean #4.2.

Of the catalyst rows I can recount from the surviving dailies, 4 read consistent and 4 read AGAINST — 50% when the data said anything at all. That is in line with last week's 56% over a larger sample.

§8 — What this says for next week

Is ×3 concentration acceptable? The evidence now cuts both ways and should be stated that way. This week it returned 1.45 against the diversified pair's 0.84; it also delivered the single worst session of the month. It is a variance choice, not a quality one. A ×2 cap would have smoothed Thursday and cost most of Monday through Wednesday.

Does the board rank? Still unproven at eight weeks — mean +0.02, stdev 0.34. But the question has sharpened: without AUD this week scores +0.81. Ask which currencies the board reads well rather than whether it reads at all.

What about AUD specifically? Board #8, traded #2.6, and the fundamentals are current — October's flash PMI confirmed the weakness on Thursday. This is not staleness. Either flow the board cannot see, or a real limit on five-day prediction from fundamentals.

What should be fixed first? Two things, both cheap. Write dailies into the repo so a retro can always recount its own week. And pull the full 28-pair grid — still the highest-value item, now five weeks old on the list.

The honest summary. A CONTESTED week with no losing pair, in which the thing I warned about twice outperformed and the thing I did not flag — a single currency the board reads backwards — cost more than either. Last week I wrote that a good week with a known failure mechanism is not yet evidence of skill. The same applies in reverse: a mediocre week whose risk fired on schedule is not evidence of failure either.
