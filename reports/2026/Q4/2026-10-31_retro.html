The Week The Board Was Right

1 WIN, 2 CONTESTED, set ratio 1.07 — the third straight step down from the clean sweep. And yet rho rose to +0.48, its best reading in six weeks: the board named CAD #1 and GBP #2 and the market agreed exactly. The ranking was right and the pairs expressing it were not. That gap is the week's subject.

§4 — The AUD anomaly, resolved

AUD has been the open item for three weeks: ranked #8 by the board, repeatedly trading near the top. The daily index-tone block added on 27 October settles it. AUD's session rank tracks the risk tone almost exactly:

The board is not mis-ranking AUD on fundamentals — it is ranking a currency whose weekly outcome is set by something the six fact tables do not contain. On 27 October AUD led with no AUD release at all (UNEXPLAINED on 65 captured rows); on 28 October it led again, and that time the calendar did explain it (inflation MoM Sep BEAT 0.8% vs 0.6%). Same rank, same gap, two different causes — which is precisely why the tone block had to be measured outside the currency board rather than inside it.

Every spread in the set compressed, and the board's rank order did not move at all. All eight currencies ended the week in the same position they started it — AUD #1 through CHF #8, unchanged — while the gaps between them narrowed: NZDCAD by 22.4% of its own width, EURUSD by 6.2%. That is the shape of a central-bank week. The ranking survived; the edge being ranked shrank. NZDCAD is the clearest case: a LOSS at 0.26 whose differential also lost a fifth of its width, so price and thesis failed together rather than disagreeing.

§6 — What this week actually taught

A right board and a flat set are not a contradiction.

rho +0.48 with a set ratio of 1.07 is the week in one line. The board ranked CAD and GBP first and second and the market agreed exactly — but CADCHF was the only pair positioned to collect it, and it was the only WIN. Ranking accuracy and pair selection are separate problems, and this week separated them cleanly for the first time.

The calendar cannot see the week's biggest mover.

AUD finished mid-table having been ranked last, and its daily rank was set by index tone rather than by any AUD release. Five central banks reported and the tone block — four instruments outside the FX board — explained more of AUD's week than the six fact tables did.

Two products disagreed usefully.

The macro set returned 1.07 and the rates set 1.01, but they failed on different pairs for different reasons: the macro side on a pair that never engaged (AUDJPY, five DRIFTs), the rates side on one whose differential was actively compressing (NZDCAD, −22.4%). Neither product would have surfaced the other's failure.
