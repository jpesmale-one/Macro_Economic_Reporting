BROAD vs NARROWCurrency strength here is one currency against the other seven, so an average can move for two very different reasons. BROAD means it moved against nearly every counterpart — the currency itself did something. NARROW means it rose against some and fell against others, and the average moved because someone else ran. HOW THIS IS READEquities rising and volatility falling is risk-on. VIX is inverted — a rise in it votes risk-off. The headline is a mean of directions, not a weighted sum, because the four markets do not share a trading window and are not on a common scale: a sum would let whichever index moved most set the tone, while counting votes asks the more robust question of how many legs agree. A leg moving less than 0.10% does not vote.
⚠ What this measure does NOT settleMeasured across every session on disk, a risk-on pair does tilt the right way — AUDJPY LONG runs 1.23 on risk-on days against 0.66 on risk-off, and NZDCHF LONG 1.27 against 0.78. But CADCHF inverts (0.93 vs 1.48), because CAD trades haven-like, not as a commodity currency: its mean strength is −0.068 on risk-on sessions and +0.071 on risk-off. So a CAD-versus-CHF trade is haven against haven, whatever the category table calls it. The role map is a closed set (F-3), so this is reported here rather than changed.
