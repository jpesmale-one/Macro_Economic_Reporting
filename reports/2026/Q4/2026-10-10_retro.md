### 1c. What can we improve

**1. Seven scoring changes landed in a week that was supposed to be frozen.**
LD-146 through LD-152 — regime-scored rate decisions, swap disclosure, the
adverse side, the ratio verdict, GBP unemployment, three-leg employment, CHF
NFP removed. Each was Jeff's call and each is defensible on its own. **Together
they mean this week's −0.62 cannot be attributed to any one of them**, and the
31 Oct review now has seven rules to separate on four weeks of data. The freeze
was the right instinct and it did not hold.

**2. Single-row categories are now a measured risk, not a theoretical one.**
AUD's board score rests on this:

| Category | Score | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Production | **−2.00** | **1** |
| Trade | **−2.00** | **1** |
| Spending | 0.00 | 4 |
| Employment | 0.00 | 3 |
| Inflation | +1.00 | 1 |

**Four points of AUD's −2.00 come from two rows.** One PMI and one trade
balance. LD-102 argued single-row categories were acceptable because they are
the norm; this week shows what the norm costs when both single rows point the
same way and the market disagrees.

**3. The model has no mechanism for the thing that actually moved price.** AUD
rose because **US Treasury yields fell from 24-year peaks** and the dollar
weakened broadly — plus RBA hike pricing falling to 30% for November. None of
that is in the whitelist, and none of it could be: the board reads released
macro data, not rate-path expectations or cross-asset flow.

**4. Two rows remain genuinely unverifiable** — USD CB Consumer Confidence and
NZD Card Spending, both bad slugs. Unchanged from last week.

### 1d. What do we continue

- **R-46 first, every Sunday.** Thirteen checks now.
- **Publishing the losing number as plainly as the winning one.** −245.0 pips
  and rho −0.62 lead this page.
- **Recording withdrawal conditions with each rule** — they are about to be
  needed.
- **Asking rather than guessing on rule edges.** LD-151's two unlisted cases
  were ruled by Jeff, not inferred by me, and the first implementation got his
  ruling backwards — caught by running the cases before committing.

**Six readings, mean ≈ −0.10.** The upward trend of the previous three weeks is
gone. **Still not significant at n=8** — rho needs ±0.71 — but the honest
summary after six weeks is that *the board has no demonstrated ranking skill*,
not that it is improving slowly.

**The base and final are identical this week**, so the calibrator question is
unresolved rather than answered.

**The specific error is the one that matters.** AUD ranked #8 and finished #1 —
a seven-rank miss, the largest the model has produced. Last week it was EUR at
#2 finishing #8. **Two consecutive weeks where the single worst-ranked
currency was at an extreme of the board.** The middle ranks are close to noise
either way; the extremes are what the set trades, and the extremes have now
been wrong twice running.

**What I would test, and it is already measurable:** whether currencies whose
base leans on *one-row categories* rank worse than those spread across several.
AUD this week and EUR last week were both extreme scores built on thin
category coverage. Five weeks of boards carry the row counts.

### 2d. What best practices do we continue

- **Grading on the day's range and the adverse side together.** The 7 BEATEN
  count said this week was contested from Monday; the close alone said it at
  the end.
- **Disclosing concentration as concentration.** The page named AUD ×3 and both
  precedents in advance. It did not prevent the loss, but it means the loss was
  not a surprise.
- **Saying what the model cannot see.** The page said AUD had no high-impact
  data scheduled. That turned out to be the whole story.

## What would change my mind

**On the method:** two consecutive extreme-rank misses in opposite directions
is the pattern to watch. A third makes the base's extremes the problem rather
than its middle.

**On single-row categories:** if the one-row test shows thin-coverage
currencies ranking worse, LD-102's "single-row categories are the norm" needs
revisiting — not by removing rows, but by **damping a category's contribution
when it rests on one input**.

**On the freeze:** seven rules changed in the week it covered. The 31 Oct
review should resolve as many as it can **and remove the ones that have ranked
nothing**, rather than carrying all seven forward on the grounds that each is
individually plausible.
