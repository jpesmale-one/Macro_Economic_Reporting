⚠ THE CONCENTRATION WAS DISCLOSED AND IT STILL COST US The page named AUD ×3 in advance, with both precedents — 0-for-3 on 14–18 Sep and 4-for-4 on 21–25 Sep. It also said AUD had no high-impact data scheduled, so the thesis rested on the prior week's prints.

All of that was correct, and none of it helped. One read expressed three times loses three times when it is wrong. The disclosure means the loss was not a surprise; it does not mean it was avoided.

1 — The report build

1a. What went well?

LD-149 called it on a week it had never seen. The ratio verdict produced 0 WIN where the old LD-128 rule would have scored 1-for-4 and the week before would have read 4-for-4. GBPAUD at 0.55 and EURAUD at 0.36 are clean LOSSes, and both closed deeply negative. The bands were fitted a week ago on data ending 28 Sep; this is their first genuine out-of-sample week.

LD-148 showed the shape. 7 BEATEN of 20 sessions, five of them on the two LOSS pairs. The grid said the bias was being contested from Monday.

R-46 reached thirteen checks and all thirteen passed before the build.

1c. What can we improve?

1. Seven scoring changes landed in a week that was supposed to be frozen. LD-146 through LD-152. Each was Jeff's call and each is defensible alone. Together they mean this week's −0.62 cannot be attributed to any one of them, and the 31 Oct review now has seven rules to separate on four weeks of data. The freeze was the right instinct and it did not hold.

2. Single-row categories are now a measured risk, not a theoretical one.

Four points of AUD's −2.00 come from two rows — one PMI and one trade balance. LD-102 argued single-row categories were acceptable because they are the norm; this week shows what the norm costs when both single rows point the same way and the market disagrees.

3. The model has no mechanism for what actually moved price. Per TE, AUD rose because “a sharp drop in US government bond yields from 24-year peaks supported the Australian dollar by weakening the US dollar broadly”, with RBA hike pricing falling to 30% for November. None of that is in the whitelist, and none of it could be: the board reads released macro data, not rate-path expectations or cross-asset flow.

4. Two rows remain genuinely unverifiable — USD CB Consumer Confidence and NZD Card Spending, both bad slugs. Unchanged.

1d. What do we continue?

R-46 first, every Sunday — thirteen checks now. Publishing the losing number as plainly as the winning one — −245.0 pips and rho −0.62 lead this page. Recording withdrawal conditions with each rule, which are about to be needed. And asking rather than guessing on rule edges: LD-151's two unlisted cases were ruled by Jeff, not inferred by me — and my first implementation got his ruling backwards, caught by running the cases before committing rather than after.

Why did it happen?

AUD rose on things the model cannot see. Per TE: “a sharp drop in US government bond yields from 24-year peaks supported the Australian dollar by weakening the US dollar broadly”, with RBA hike pricing falling to 30% for November. AUDUSD rose 0.37% — its first weekly gain in five weeks.

Monday did most of the damage, before any scheduled release. GBPAUD −104 adverse, EURAUD −136 adverse (1.74× ATR), AUDCHF BEATEN — three of four pairs hit on day one. That is a positioning move, not a data move.

⚠ THE HONEST SUMMARY AFTER SIX WEEKS Six readings, mean about −0.10. The upward trend of the previous three weeks is gone. Still not significant at n=8 — rho needs ±0.71 — but the fair statement is that the board has no demonstrated ranking skill, not that it is improving slowly. Base and final are identical this week, so the calibrator question is unresolved rather than answered.

The specific error is the one that matters. AUD ranked #8 and finished #1 — a seven-rank miss, the largest the model has produced. Last week it was EUR at #2 finishing #8. Two consecutive weeks where the worst-ranked currency sat at an extreme of the board. The middle ranks are close to noise either way; the extremes are what the set trades, and they have now been wrong twice running.

What I would test, and it is already measurable: whether currencies whose base leans on one-row categories rank worse than those spread across several. AUD this week and EUR last week were both extreme scores built on thin category coverage. Five weeks of boards carry the row counts.

2d. What best practices do we continue?

Grading the day's range and the adverse side together — the 7 BEATEN count said this week was contested from Monday; the close said it only at the end. Disclosing concentration as concentration. And saying what the model cannot see — the page said AUD had no high-impact data scheduled, and that turned out to be the whole story.

What would change my mind

On the method: two consecutive extreme-rank misses in opposite directions is the pattern to watch. A third makes the base's extremes the problem rather than its middle.

On single-row categories: if the one-row test shows thin-coverage currencies ranking worse, LD-102's “single-row categories are the norm” needs revisiting — not by removing rows, but by damping a category's contribution when it rests on one input.

On the freeze: seven rules changed in the week it covered. The 31 Oct review should resolve as many as it can and remove the ones that have ranked nothing, rather than carrying all seven forward because each is individually plausible.
