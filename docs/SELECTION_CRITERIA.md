## 1. Strong vs weak — but NOT mechanically top-vs-bottom

> *"The Idea is to match stronger currencies vs weaker currencies. But I did
> not give a hard rule that we should immediately match top vs bottom.
> Ideally this is the best bet but if Top 8 for example have high impact news
> on the upcoming week then there's really no way of telling where the price
> would go."*  — **2026-08-29**

**The macro spread RANKS candidates. It does not select them.**

## 2. The forecast week is part of the decision

> *"aside from Macro indicator we should be considerring the forecasted news
> when selecting a pair. I'm not saying also that we should avoid top vs
> bottom with upcoming high impact news its such that we need to be more
> careful with these kind of set-ups."*  — **2026-08-29**

> *"With this we can foresee if the behavior of that currency when the market
> is pricing this week. This should be considered when we're determining our
> Proposed High Probability Pairs"*  — **2026-08-24**

**Event risk DOWNGRADES confidence; it never vetoes.** A crowded pair must
earn its place on room.

## 3. Interest rate direction is the anchor

> *"The increase in the Interest Rate means Good for the currency because it
> will lessen the supply of that currency. Meaning the value will go up. The
> opposite can be said for cutting interest rates. … So if a currency
> increased an interest rate automatically that currency is strong."*
> — **2026-08-21**

> *"Production, Spending and Trade tends to predict the result of Employment
> and Inflation. And Employment and Inflation tends to predict what would be
> the Interest rate decision. Because the Interest Rate dictates if the
> Currency is Strong or Weak. That's the whole idea."*  — **2026-08-29**

**This is the causal chain §2.0b measures (LD-72).**

## 4. ROOM TO MOVE — the technical gate

> *"Once you have a candidate pair we can check if the price is still have the
> bandwidth to move. for example GBPUSD and our bias is Long (favoring GBP)
> but we see its already near resistance there's a very good chance that it
> would reverse first before continuing the upside or completely reverse. In
> such cases we may want to look for other pairs with the same or a little bit
> lesser conviction than GBPUSD but with room to move in the favor of our
> bias."*  — **2026-08-29**

> *"Check their Support and Resistance zones on D and H4 timeframes. Draw
> these zones in Trading View OANDA exchange"*  — **2026-08-20**

**A LESSER SPREAD WITH ROOM BEATS A BETTER SPREAD WITHOUT IT.** That is the
rule in one line, and it is the rule F-69 found unapplied on the bench.

**This applies to EVERY pair we might enter — primary and secondary alike.**

## 5. No correlated legs

> *"CADJPY, GBPJPY, and EURJPY are all co-related pairs. Since JPY's bias
> held, All 3 rise at the same time but if the other way around happened then
> all 3 would be doomed as well. Next time let's find Non-corelated pairs so
> that we can truly see the result of our analysis and pinpoint where we can
> improve."*  — **2026-08-21**

**Leg-disjoint (LD-19).** The purpose is DIAGNOSTIC as much as protective —
three correlated pairs are one bet reported as three.

## 6. Three is a FLOOR, not a target — and secondaries are a bench

> *"the 3 pairs is just the Minimum I asked. But it doesn't mean we can't go
> more than 3. As long as we have high conviction without correlation to the
> other selected ones then we can still add them"*  — **2026-08-22**

> *"try to find 3 at least then just add your confidence level so that the
> user would have an Idea what to focus at. Remember we're not implying these
> are guaranteed win. We just saw these pairs as High Probability trades based
> on our Fundamental and Technical Analysis."*  — **2026-08-21**

> *"I'd say let's work first with the highest conviction pairs that you see.
> But just like what we did for 4.5 Correlated we place secondary pairs here.
> … Then as the trading day goes by if things go south for the 1st selection
> then we start looking our secondary pairs"*  — **2026-08-30**

**“If things go south” is governed by the falsifier (LD-29), not by feel.**
