INTEREST RATE SCORING - THE RULE, AND THE EVIDENCE BEHIND IT
Recorded 2026-10-12 at Jeff's request: "document the new rule as an evidence
in Idea_dump so there is something we can go back to"

This file exists because on 2026-10-12 Jeff asked "where did you get this
rule?" about the Interest Rate scoring and COULD NOT CHECK THE ANSWER. Every
other category's rule was written in Macro_economic_indicators.txt; the rate
rule lived only in code comments and git history. He was then right that the
rule he found there was wrong. Both halves of that are why this document
exists: the rule is recorded, AND the primary sources are quoted, so the next
disagreement is settled from the record instead of from memory.

Everything below is quoted verbatim or cited by commit hash. Where this file
states a conclusion, the evidence for it is directly beneath.


================================================================================
1. THE LIVE RULE
================================================================================

ONE TERM. The Interest Rate row is scored on WHAT THE BANK IS EXPECTED TO DO
NEXT. Nothing is averaged against it.

Jeff, 2026-10-10, verbatim (commit 7d99dd7):

    BEFORE THE NEWS                   AFTER THE NEWS
      Forecast > Previous = Good        Actual = Forecast = retain
      Forecast < Previous = Bad         Actual > Forecast = override Good
      Forecast = Previous = Neutral     Actual < Forecast = override Bad

His reasoning, verbatim:

    "This way we can reflect the forward looking sentiment the soonest on
     Before the news scenario and ride the decision on the After the news
     scenario."

CONSENSUS SUPERSEDES FORECAST. Jeff, 2026-10-10, verbatim:

    "Forecast is fine to use but when consensus appeared we should rescore it
     based on that."

  WHY BOTH ARE READ RATHER THAN CONSENSUS ALONE - measured, not assumed:
  across 140 rate rows in our captures, consensus is present on only 42% and
  arrives a MEDIAN ~4 DAYS ahead (ECB 6, BoE 4, Fed 3, RBA 3). A
  consensus-only rule would fire about a week out instead of from the moment
  the decision is scheduled. The field `src` records which number scored the
  row, so a rescore is visible rather than silent.

A CURRENCY WITH NO SCHEDULED DECISION carries its LAST decision forward
(LD-154b). This is what takes the term from 3 of 8 currencies to all 8.

  WHY IT IS NEEDED - against the real 4 Oct calendar, TE had the Fed and ECB
  rows COMPLETELY EMPTY (no consensus, no forecast, ECB no previous either)
  and no October row at all for GBP, AUD or CHF. Without the carry-forward the
  only forward-looking term in the model was inert for most of the board.

  Every carried decision is FULLY PRICED (actual == consensus), so each one
  RETAINS its score rather than being re-paid for happening. That is LD-90,
  Jeff 2026-09-02: "A central bank decision moves price by its surprise
  component only. Level of rates -> already discounted."

  Supersession is automatic: the moment TE schedules the next decision with a
  number, the scheduled row is read and the currency flips to before-news. No
  manual switch.


================================================================================
2. THE 1.5-2.5% CORRIDOR IS NOT PART OF THIS RULE, AND NEVER WAS
================================================================================

Jeff, 2026-10-12, verbatim:

    "Then I don't think this is correct. I gave the 1.5-2.5 range to Inflation
     as I remember because for IR our concern is the change not on a
     particular target"
    "I'd say lets stick to the new rule I gave you. Let's strip down all the
     rules we have in Interest Rate and apply the new rule purely"

HIS MEMORY IS CORRECT. THE EVIDENCE:

  THE ONLY PLACE JEFF HIMSELF NAMES 1.5-2.5 IS ABOUT INFLATION.
  Jeff, 2026-09-21, on JPY CPI printing 1.9% after 1.9% (commit 3bc3212):

      "This should be Neutral since 1.9 is in between 1.5 to 2.5"

  That is a CPI print. There is no quotation anywhere in the repo of Jeff
  applying that band to a policy rate.

  THE TWO COMMITS ARE 38 MINUTES 36 SECONDS APART, and the order shows how the band
  spread:

      3423bf8   2026-09-20 21:33:12   LD-142 puts 1.5-2.5 on the POLICY RATE
      3bc3212   2026-09-20 22:11:48   LD-143 carries it to INFLATION

  LD-143's own commit message names the motive, verbatim:

      "THE REAL GAIN IS THAT THE TWO RULES BECOME ONE. LD-142 introduced the
       corridor for the POLICY RATE a day earlier. Both categories now use an
       IDENTICAL BAND"

  THAT SYMMETRY WAS BUILT BECAUSE ONE BAND FOR BOTH WAS TIDIER. Tidiness is
  not a reason. The asymmetry is real and it is the whole point:

      INFLATION HAS A 2% TARGET.   A corridor around it is meaningful.
      A POLICY RATE HAS NO TARGET. A corridor around it asserts a neutral
                                   level that no central bank has declared.

WHAT THE CORRIDOR WAS ACTUALLY DOING, measured on the live board 2026-10-12.
Three of eight currencies were scored on where the rate SAT rather than on
what the bank DID:

      GBP   3.75%   HELD     ->  +1     a positive sign for doing nothing
      CHF   0.00%   HELD     ->  -1     a negative sign for doing nothing
      JPY   1.25%   HIKED    ->   0     an actual hike, scored as nothing

All three now read 0, because none of them changed anything the market had not
already priced. GBP and CHF being SIGNED FOR HOLDING was a direct violation of
LD-90 - the principle the corridor was quietly working against.


================================================================================
3. WHAT WAS RETIRED, AND WHY EACH ONE WENT
================================================================================

The rate model has carried five terms at different times. FOUR ARE RETIRED.
They are kept in the code at weight 0.0 rather than deleted, so a stale caller
fails loudly instead of silently scoring zero.

  level      the 1.5-2.5 corridor test.
             RETIRED LD-166, 2026-10-12, for the reasons in section 2.

  realised   paid +1 for having hiked AT ALL, regardless of whether the hike
             was priced. RETIRED LD-163, 2026-10-11 (commit 9c5fd7b), on
             Jeff's "we should follow now the new IR scoring. the one I gave
             you."
             WHY: it double-counted. On 11 Oct the September hikes were paying
             USD, EUR and AUD through `realised` while `path` separately
             reported them as fully priced and worth nothing extra. JPY was
             the clearest case - +0.20 of its score came purely from having
             hiked, on a level of 0 and a path of 0.

  expected   CPI scored against each central bank's own mandate band.
             RETIRED LD-142, 2026-09-20.
             WHY: it was a PROXY for the rate tone, and it double-counted -
             CPI already reaches the board through the Inflation category.

  surprise   RETIRED on Jeff's instruction, 2026-09-20, verbatim:
             "the surprise won't matter here anymore. because by the time we
              capture the data its already past."

ALSO SUPERSEDED, for the record:

  LD-140     scored consensus-vs-standing - whether a move was EXPECTED. On
             18 Sep it read JPY +1 for a priced hike IN THE WEEK THE YEN FELL
             1.58%, the worst of the eight. Jeff: "This needs correction."
             Replaced, not kept alongside: one rule, one quantity.

  LD-141     a proposal to score the CPI gap from 2%. Superseded before
             application - LD-154 reads the decision itself, which is the
             quantity LD-141 was approximating.

4. THE RULE APPLIED - THE LIVE BOARD, 2026-10-12
================================================================================

Worked from the real data so the arithmetic can be checked against this file.

  CCY   rate      prev      score    what scored it
  USD   4.00%     3.75%     +1.00    AFTER - 4% exactly as expected, retain
  EUR   2.65%     2.40%     +1.00    AFTER - 2.65% as expected, retain
  AUD   4.60%     4.35%     +1.00    AFTER - 4.6% as expected, retain
  NZD   2.75%     2.50%      0.00    BEFORE - expected HOLD at 2.75%
  GBP   3.75%     3.75%      0.00    AFTER - 3.75% as expected, retain
  JPY   1.25%     1.00%      0.00    BEFORE - expected HOLD at 1.25%
  CAD   2.25%     2.25%      0.00    BEFORE - expected HOLD at 2.25%
  CHF   0.00%     0.00%      0.00    AFTER - 0% as expected, retain

  READ IT THIS WAY: USD, EUR and AUD score +1 because each HIKED and the
  decision is being retained - they moved the rate up and the market got what
  it expected, so the hawkish reading stands. NZD, JPY and CAD score 0 because
  a HOLD is expected at their next meeting. GBP and CHF score 0 because their
  last decisions were holds, fully priced.

  *** NOTE WHAT IS NO LONGER HAPPENING. Under the retired corridor, GBP read
  +1 and CHF read -1 on these exact same numbers - purely because 3.75% sits
  above 2.5% and 0.00% sits below 1.5%. Neither bank had done anything.

5. WHERE THIS LIVES IN CODE, AND WHAT GUARDS IT
================================================================================

  pipelines/rate_model.py
      decision_score()         the rule in section 1, before and after
      LAST / standing_score()  LD-154b, the carry-forward
      TERM_WEIGHT              path 1.00; the four retired terms 0.0
      level_score()            STILL PRESENT for the audit trail, WEIGHT 0.
                               It must never reach the score.

  pipelines/test_inflation.py   R-44, the LD-166 block asserts:
      - TERM_WEIGHT level is 0.0
      - TERM_WEIGHT path is 1.0, the sole term
      - GBP, CHF and JPY all read 0 (the three the corridor scored wrong)
      - raw == path for all eight, so nothing is averaged in
    Negative-tested 2026-10-12: restoring level to 0.50 fails all five checks.

  A LESSON WORTH KEEPING. Four separate times a rate rule changed and the
  REPORT kept describing the old one (F-135, F-148, F-149, F-150). The rate
  row is scored OUTSIDE score_model, where most data guards look, so a
  renderer/rule mismatch there is invisible to them. When this rule changes
  again, THE TABLE COLUMNS AND THE FOOTNOTE MUST CHANGE IN THE SAME PASS.
